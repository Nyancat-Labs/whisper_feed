## What Whisper is

A reader. You give it addresses of feeds; it fetches them and shows you what
they contain. It is the same relationship a web browser has with the websites
you type into it.

## What Whisper is not

**It is not a publisher, and it does not choose your sources.** Every feed in
the app is one you added — by typing an address, importing an OPML file,
scanning your bookmarks, or accepting a suggestion you were shown and could
decline. Whisper ships with a short starter list you may take or leave, and
which you can remove entirely.

**We do not host, mirror, or redistribute anyone's content.** Articles are
fetched by your device, from the publisher, and stored on your device for you
to read. Nothing passes through a server of ours, because there isn't one.

**We do not endorse, verify, moderate or fact-check anything a feed contains.**
An article appearing in Whisper says only that a feed you subscribed to
published it. Accuracy, legality and tone are the publisher's, and judging them
is yours.

## Content and copyright

Articles, images and feed content belong to whoever made them, and their terms
apply. Whisper's licence covers Whisper — the reader — and nothing that arrives
through it.

Feeds are published in order to be fetched by readers, and that is all the app
does with them. Full-text fetching, which is off unless you turn it on, requests
the article's own page and keeps a reading copy on your device — the same page a
browser would load, for you to read once, on your own phone. It sends no
cookies and no account, so material behind a paywall or a login stays there. It
is not a way around either, and using it to redistribute anything is not
something the app supports or that we condone.

If you publish a feed and would rather Whisper did not fetch it, the ordinary
web mechanisms work: Whisper identifies itself in its `User-Agent` on every
request, so you can identify and block it as you would any other client.
